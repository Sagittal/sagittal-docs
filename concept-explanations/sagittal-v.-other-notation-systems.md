# Sagittal v. other notation systems

Sagittal is not the only microtonal notation, and it does not claim to be the best one for every job. What sets it apart is its **scope**: most systems are built for one tuning or one family of tunings, while Sagittal aims to notate almost any tuning *and* keep the notation of a given harmony consistent as you move between tunings. This page places Sagittal next to the systems you are most likely to have met.

{% hint style="info" %}
The honest framing: if you only ever work in one tuning and a notation was designed for it, you may well prefer that notation. Sagittal's payoff is for people who move *between* tunings, or who want one set of principles underneath all of them.
{% endhint %}

## Reading the symbols: three mnemonics

Before the comparisons, a word on why Sagittal's symbols are quick to learn. Each one shows you two things at a glance — the **direction** of the alteration (which way the arrow points) and its **relative size** (how much symbol there is). On top of that, the flags carry mnemonics for their prime:

## Johnston

Ben Johnston's notation assigns accidentals to raise or lower by specific ratios from a justly-tuned C major scale. It is elegant for extended just intonation but is anchored to that one starting scale, and its accidentals do not visually encode the size or direction of their alteration the way sagittals do. Cam Taylor transcribed the third movement of Johnston's String Quartet No. 10 into both Evo and Revo Sagittal (playing back at correct pitch), which makes a good side-by-side study of the two approaches. <!-- TODO: link the transcription images when the asset pass runs -->

## Helmholtz–Ellis (HEJI) and Prime Factor Sagittal

Helmholtz–Ellis Just Intonation (HEJI, once written "EHEJIPN") notates JI with one accidental per prime, extending the Helmholtz–Ellis tradition. Sagittal is itself, in a real sense, *an extended Helmholtz–Ellis notation* — and its [Prime Factor notation](../notation-how-to-guides/prime-factor.md) is the Sagittal that most closely resembles HEJI and Johnston: one sagittal per prime factor, combined freely. If you are coming from HEJI, Prime Factor Sagittal is the natural door in.

## Stein–Zimmermann and the quartertone notations

The familiar quartertone accidentals — Stein's semisharp, Zimmermann's reversed flats — are the ones Sagittal was consciously designed to improve upon (the *Gift of the Gods* story dramatizes exactly this choice). Sagittal does not discard them: the **Evo-SZ** sub-flavor keeps the Stein–Zimmermann half-sharps and half-flats where they are entrenched, substituting them for the corresponding sagittals in tempered contexts.

## Tall Kite's color notation

Kite Giedraitis's "color" notation labels alterations with lowercase-letter-and-number codes (with color-name mnemonics for the most common few). It and Sagittal describe the same intervals, and a direct correspondence table exists — for the common thirds, for example:

| Ratio | Color | Sagittal (short) | Sagispeak |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7/6 | azure `z` | `t` | tao |
| 6/5 | green `g` | `/` | pai |
| 11/9 | lovender `1o` | | |
| 5/4 | yellow `y` | `\` | pao |
| 9/7 | red `r` | `f` | tai |

The systems make different bets: color notation leans on letters and rainbow order, while a sagittal already shows direction and size in its shape and carries its own prime mnemonics. Neither is "correct" — they translate cleanly, and which reads better is a matter of what you are used to.

## Melodic and proportional notations

Finally, some approaches step outside the staff entirely — proportional or spatial notations (David Canright's rational notation, Andrew Killick's global notation) place pitch by position rather than by symbol. And some musical traditions are primarily *melodic* rather than harmonic — the Sargam system of Hindustani and Karnatic music names scale degrees, not harmonies. To use Sagittal is to buy into a five-line staff and seven nominals, with assumptions that are primarily harmonic; for a deeply melodic tradition, Sagittal may be a complement rather than a replacement. That limit is discussed on [Why Sagittal](../why-sagittal.md).
