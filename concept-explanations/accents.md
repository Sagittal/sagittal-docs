# Accents

For most music, a [flag-and-shaft](symbol-elements.md) sagittal — accurate to within about 2¢ — is plenty. **Accents** are for when 2¢ is not good enough. They are small diacritics that add fractions of a cent of resolution, introduced by the three outermost [symbol sets](symbol-sets.md).

## Where they go, and how big

An accent always attaches to a **core** — a sagittal, or a bare shaft when no sagittal is present — never on its own, and never on a conventional sharp or flat.

Accents represent smaller alterations than flags, so, by the **smallest-to-the-left** principle, they sit to the *left* of the core and are drawn smaller (less ink for a smaller alteration). Unlike flags, an accent may point in a **different direction** from the core, and two accents on one symbol may point differently from each other — with the smaller alteration always the leftmost.

## Knowing when to drop them

Here is the part that matters most, and the part beginners get backward: with accents, **knowing when to leave one off is more important than knowing when to add one.**

An accent earns its place only when your music actually draws a distinction finer than the un-accented symbol can express. If it does not, the accent is noise — it makes the page harder to read and the notation harder to sight-sing, for no gain. A score can even declare, in its legend, that a given accent should be *dropped* — effectively redefining the symbol's value for that piece. Reach for an accent when a real pitch distinction demands it, and drop it the moment it stops earning its keep.
