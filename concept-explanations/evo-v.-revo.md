# The Two Flavors: Evo & Revo

Every Sagittal notation comes in two **flavors**. They answer one question: *how do you use Sagittal without the conventional sharp and flat?*

* **Evo** (from *evolutionary*) keeps the conventional sharps and flats and evolves the notation by adding single-shaft sagittals alongside them.
* **Revo** (from *revolutionary*) throws out sharps and flats entirely, replacing them with **multi-shaft** sagittals — one accidental per note.

The flavors are **equally preferred** by the Sagittal community — each co-creator favored a different one — and both are fully valid. But they must never be mixed on the same score. If you see a conventional sharp or flat, you should never see a multi-shaft sagittal. (Think chocolate and garlic: both great, not together.)

{% hint style="info" %}
These flavors were formerly called **Mixed** (Evo) and **Pure** (Revo), and you will still meet the old names in older forum discussions. Same two things, two pairs of names.
{% endhint %}

## Evo first, as a stepping-stone

We teach Evo first, because it leans on notation you already know. It is exactly the pattern you met with 72-EDO in the introduction: once an alteration grows past a certain point, you write a conventional sharp and then a **downward** sagittal to walk part of the way back. Simple to pick up — but it means two symbols on the note, pointing in opposite directions.

Revo takes more up-front learning (a new set of multi-shaft symbols to absorb) but pays you back with a simpler page: one symbol per note, and never any tug-of-war between an up and a down.

## Revo is a substitution code for Evo

Line the two flavors up, natural to sharp, and for most of the range they are **identical** — the same single-shaft sagittals. They part ways only at the top, where Evo writes a conventional sharp and Revo writes a multi-shaft symbol. So a Revo multi-shaft symbol is really just a **substitution code** for an Evo combination of a sharp with a single-shaft sagittal.

Adding a sharp, in Revo, means adding two shafts. Its own sharp — the "double-shaft double barb" `/||\` — is worth memorizing; you will see it constantly. It helps to picture the four strokes of the conventional sharp morphing into the four strokes of the Sagittal one.

Beyond that come triple-shaft and **ex-shaft** (four-shaft, drawn as an "X") symbols, carrying you up to the double sharp. Past the double sharp lies what Sagittal fondly calls "the edge of the world" — here be dragons. You *could* keep adding shafts, but Sagittal neither describes nor recommends it: if you are that far from a note, you should probably be spelling from a different note.

{% hint style="info" %}
Dave's maxim for Revo, the "Pure" flavor: **"If you can't do it in Pure Sagittal, it doesn't exist."** Whatever a score needs, there is a way to write it with one Revo symbol per note.
{% endhint %}

## Symbol order

A standing rule across both flavors:

> **Larger alterations go closest to the notehead.**

This is the same **smallest-to-the-left** principle that keeps a single-shaft sagittal farther from the note than a conventional sharp in Evo, and that places [accents](accents.md) to the left of their core. Read outward from the notehead and the alterations shrink.

## The 60% rule

You might expect single-shaft sagittals to stop exactly at the half-apotome (50%), handing everything above to a sharp-plus-sagittal pair. They actually reach a bit further — about **60%** — before the sharp takes over. The extra 10% is a deliberate courtesy: reaching just past halfway with a *single* symbol is such a common need that it is worth avoiding the opposing-symbol pair a little longer. Every 2,3-free ratio has two commas an [apotome](apotome-complements.md) apart, so there is always a single-shaft symbol available to cover that stretch.

## Evo-SZ

There is a sub-flavor of Evo worth knowing. In a tempered system where a sagittal comes out to *exactly* a half-sharp or half-flat, **Evo-SZ** swaps in the Stein–Zimmermann accidentals instead — the familiar semisharp and (narrow) reversed flat. The narrow variants preserve size-proportionality and cut down on left/right confusion. They are not Sagittal symbols, but they substitute cleanly for them.
