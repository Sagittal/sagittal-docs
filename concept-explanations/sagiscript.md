# Sagiscript

You will not always have a music font handy. Sometimes you need a sagittal on paper — sketching an idea, marking up a part, teaching at a whiteboard. **Sagiscript** is the handwritten form of Sagittal: how to draw the symbols by hand so they stay legible and unambiguous.

{% hint style="info" %}
Three plain-text cousins, three jobs: [Sagispeak](sagispeak.md) is for **saying** a sagittal, [Sagitype](sagitype.md) is for **typing** it, and Sagiscript is for **writing** it by hand.
{% endhint %}

## The one thing that matters: keep the four flags distinct

Every sagittal is built from [symbol elements](symbol-elements.md) — a shaft plus one or more flags — and there are four flag types. The whole trick to handwriting Sagittal is keeping those four **visually distinct** from one another, because that distinction is what carries the meaning. This is exactly where beginners struggle: rushed by hand, all four flags start to look alike.

The reliable fix is a **squared-up** teaching method: picture each flag first as an angular, straightened-out skeleton of its curve, then let your pen round it off toward the printed glyph — the barb staying straight, the arc bulging outward, the scroll curling inward, and the boathook flicking into a little hook. Exaggerate the convex and concave a bit as you write, the way a calligrapher would, and the four never collapse into one another.

## A memory aid worth stealing

Mnemonics help the shapes stick. A favorite comes from Japanese: the 13 large-diesis symbol `(|\` (jakai) looks like **大**, *dai*, meaning "big" — a perfect fit for the biggest of the common single-shaft alterations, and a happy accident that it is spelled *dai* in short Sagitype too. Once you see it, you never forget which one is the large one. (For a 14:13, that makes it a *janai* second — a small middle second, not a plain minor second.)

## Your handwriting is allowed to have a style

There is no single "correct" drawing of a sagittal, only the requirement that the flags read clearly. Sagittal's own creators expected the glyphs to evolve as more hands and more font designers interpreted them — third-party fonts already exist (Thomas Richter's set for the Ekmelily LilyPond package, for one) that draw the strokes in their own style. So your Sagiscript can carry your own hand, as long as a reader can still tell a barb from an arc from a scroll from a boathook.
