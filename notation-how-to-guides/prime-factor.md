# Prime Factor Notation

Prime Factor notation is the first of Sagittal's two just intonation notations. It is the easier of the two to understand, so we teach it first — but it is the *other* JI notation that Sagittal prefers, which is why that one earns the plain name "JI notation." If you only want the preferred notation, you can skip ahead. Prime Factor is worth knowing anyway, so stick around.

{% hint style="info" %}
Prime Factor notation is the Sagittal notation that most closely resembles other popular microtonal JI notations, such as HEJI (Helmholtz-Ellis) and Ben Johnston's notation. If you already know one of those, you will feel at home here.
{% endhint %}

## One sagittal per prime factor

The whole idea fits in a sentence: **for every prime factor in your ratio above 3, add the sagittal for that prime.** Move by a 5-limit comma, add a 5-comma sagittal. Move by a 7-limit comma, add a 7-comma sagittal. And so on.

Conventional notation already handles the primes 2 and 3 — that is what the staff, the nominals, and the sharps and flats are for. So a sagittal is only ever needed for the primes 5, 7, 11, 13, and up.

## Learn a direction for each prime harmonic

The cleanest way to learn the symbols is straight from the harmonic series, with no mention of commas at all. Harmonics are real and otonal chords are common, while subharmonics are rare — so you just learn one **symbol direction per prime harmonic**:

Three of the first four primes (5, 7, 13) sit just *below* their nearest Pythagorean note, so they take a downward symbol; only the 11th harmonic sits *above*, so it takes an upward one. Learn C-to-E lowered by `\!` is the just major third 4:5, C-to-F raised by `/|\` is 8:11, and the rest follows.

The first three symbols you already know from the [starter set](../fundamentals/introduction-to-sagittal.md). Only the 13 symbol is new. It looks a bit like the 11-comma — flags on both sides — except one of them is curved, so it also looks like a cross between the 5- and 7-comma symbols.

{% hint style="info" %}
With just these four symbols you can exactly notate **any** pitch in the 13-limit. Rooted on G, the whole 13-limit harmonic stack (the "13th chord" from the homepage diagram) needs only sagittals — no sharps or flats at all. The only limit is how many symbols you are willing to pile onto one note.
{% endhint %}

## Going higher than the 13-limit

Past the 13-limit you begin to need symbols beyond the [Spartan set](../concept-explanations/symbol-sets.md), but the principle never changes: one sagittal per prime. Symbols have been worked out up to the 61st harmonic; if you somehow need to go further, there is a documented process for deriving the correct symbol, and the community would love for you to share what you find on the [forum](http://forum.sagittal.org). But you almost certainly do not need primes past 61 — humans cannot really hear harmony at that resolution anyway.

## Its one drawback

Prime Factor notation is simple, but it can get cluttered. Because each prime factor gets its own symbol, a single note can end up carrying **several** sagittals — and sometimes they even point in opposite directions. Notating 7/5, for example, wants a downward 7 and an upward 5 on the same note; 10/7 is the mirror.

That is completely valid Sagittal notation. But the co-creators wanted a JI notation with **one accidental per note and no opposing alterations**, and that is exactly what the other JI notation delivers. Sometimes multiple prime-factor sagittals even collapse into a single symbol: `!)` plus `/|\` comes out close enough to `|)` to be written as just `|)`. That kind of consolidation is the whole point of the preferred notation.
