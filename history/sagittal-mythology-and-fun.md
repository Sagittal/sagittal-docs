# Sagittal Mythology & Fun

Sagittal has a serious side — decades of careful theory — and a thoroughly unserious one. Its creators were, among other things, practical jokers, and the system arrived in the world wrapped in a running mythology.

## Gift of the Gods and the Hermes hoax

When the Sagittal website officially opened in **2004** ("The Sagittal website is officially open," ran the announcement), it came with an unfinished mythology called **Gift of the Gods** — a tongue-in-cheek origin story framing the notation as a gift handed down from on high. The conceit was that the whole thing had been written by the Greek god **Hermes**, who claimed to have been *impersonating* "George Secor" all along.

The bit paid off in October 2007. The **real** George Secor turned up at a 19-tone concert (the Midwest Microfest) and cheerfully claimed to know nothing of the Yahoo tuning groups or of Sagittal — leaving the community briefly, delightfully confused. The mythology's final episode followed, and a small flurry of forum threads worked through the aftermath with titles like "Sagittal mythology getting out of hand?", "Why the hoax?", "What hoax??", and "Will the REAL George Secor please stand up?"

## The story the myth tells

*Gift of the Gods* is worth reading in full (it lives at [sagittal.org/gift](https://sagittal.org/gift/), narrated by "Hermes" and scored with George's own period performances). Its charm is that every plot beat is a real design decision in disguise:

* A revived Mount Olympus wants to win back worshippers from the tuning community's kindred spirits — the "yahoos" of the Yahoo tuning list. Their gift, after Hermes surveys the archives, is the one thing everyone argues about: **notation, notation, and notation.**
* An Elysian committee — Pythagoras, Plato, Didymus — settles the ground rules that are still Sagittal's: keep the conventional staff and seven nominals; define every symbol as a **rational comma** modifying a chain of fifths, so a symbol keeps its harmonic meaning across tunings.
* **Apollo** fires a golden arrow into the committee table to decree the single-shaft arrow as the **11-diesis** — "one quarter-step for a tone, one giant leap for tonality!" A second arrow beside it gives the two-shaft flat; crossed arrows from Apollo and Artemis give the double-sharp. **Zeus** takes the apotome (a lightning bolt); **Hera** keeps the natural (it cancels, as she avenges).
* **Artemis**, showing off, cleaves Apollo's arrow in two — and the resulting half-arrow becomes Didymus' **5-comma** flag, improving on Bosanquet's confusable slashes. (Hence the "dibbler" of the first episode's title.) The rule that symbols invert by **vertical mirroring**, never rotation, is set here too.
* When Dave-the-character objects that mirror-image symbols are confusable, the fix is a new flag *shape*: Plato's friend **Archytas** holds a bow arched over his head, and the **arc** flag — a rainbow of seven colors — is born for the **7-comma**. Straight flags are named **barbs**, curved ones **arcs**.
* Because a few members (notably Pehrson and Keenan) resist replacing sharps and flats, the notation is offered in two forms — **pure** and **mixed**, the ancestors of today's [Revo and Evo](../concept-explanations/evo-v.-revo.md).
* More gods, more symbols: **Dionysus** is bought off with the two-handled-wine-cup diesis `(|)` (the apotome-complement of Apollo's 11-diesis, "just as Apollo and Dionysus complement one another as the rational and the ecstatic"); **Athene** and **Ares** get the neutral-third helmets `/|)` and `(|\`; **Artemis** is thanked with the reinstated 55-comma `|\`.

The final episode closes the loop with a wink: the Muses plant in *Dave Keenan's* head the idea that "Secor" should write a tutorial *as* a mythology — so the story ends by commissioning itself.

## The Periodic Table and "Dmitri Mendeleev"

The tradition of the publicity prank continued to the very end of George and Dave's collaboration.

The year **2019** was the International Year of the Periodic Table, marking the 150th anniversary of Dmitri Mendeleev's Periodic Table of the Elements — which the [Periodic Table of EDOs](../concept-explanations/the-periodic-table-of-edos.md) was designed to resemble. That November, Dave organized a prank for George's enjoyment: **"Dmitri Mendeleev himself"** popped up in the Facebook group *Microtonal Music and Tuning Theory*, complete with Hollywood-Russian bad English, and presented the Periodic Table of EDOs to the world.

It became something more than a joke. In Dave's words:

> "It was the last thing that George and I did together. George passed away 4 months later. It was the end of an 18 year collaboration. Thank you for all of it, George." — Dave Keenan

## A Bit O' Blarney

The playfulness ran through George's own writing too. Shortly after he and Dave "met" on the tuning list in 2002, George posted a comic tale about a character named **Justin Tenacious** (say it quickly). New to online forums, George was crestfallen when it drew zero replies — until Dave reassured him: people only respond when there's an argument to be had, and "you can't argue with perfection."

That spirit — rigorous theory delivered with a wink — is part of Sagittal's character, and worth preserving alongside the technical record.
