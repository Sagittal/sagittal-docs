# George Secor: A Memorial

**George D. Secor** (November 1943 – March 2, 2020) was a microtonal composer, theorist, and keyboard virtuoso, and the originating genius behind Sagittal. He died on March 2, 2020, aged 76, after a long illness, in Godfrey, Illinois. He was survived by his daughter Samara; his wife Shirley had died in 2013.

{% hint style="info" %}
His surname is pronounced **"SEE-kor."**
{% endhint %}

## The musician

George was a virtuoso of the keyboard — both conventional and generalized microtonal keyboards — and a lifelong explorer of tuning. Those who knew him remember a man with a heart of gold and a wicked sense of humor: "a wicked practical joker," in Dave Keenan's phrase. That humor runs all through Sagittal's history, from the [Hermes hoax](sagittal-mythology-and-fun.md) to his own comic writings.

His 2009 xenharmonic masterpiece, **"Coming on Clouds"** — composed in 16 notes of a high-tolerance temperament (29-HTT) — is playable on [sagittal.org](https://sagittal.org) and is a fine place to hear his musical mind at work.

## The secor

George's name lives on in a unit of interval measure. The **secor** is the generator of **Miracle temperament** — roughly the size of a 16:15 minor second — and George first derived it in a **1975 article in *Xenharmonikôn***, without naming it.

The name came later, and not from George. Dave Keenan proposed calling it the "secor," partly for its mnemonic value: it is a **sec**ond, min**or** — **SEC-OR** — and partly to honor its discoverer.

{% hint style="info" %}
Dave has always been careful to note that the secor was **named after George, not by him**: "I wouldn't want people to think he was so vain." The community rediscovered the temperament years later, tracked George down through his 1975 article, and that is what drew him onto the tuning list in the first place.
{% endhint %}

## Beaux Eaux

A small, characteristic story. While George and Dave were making Sagispeak language-independent, George shared that his fast-food alias was **"Beaux Eaux"** — French for "beautiful waters," pronounced simply "Bozo." Proud of his French ancestry, he offered it as both a joke and a pronunciation lesson. When George died, Dave signed off: "I'm going to miss you, Beaux Eaux."

## Never met, deeply known

George and Dave collaborated for eighteen years and **never met in person** — the entire creation of Sagittal passed between Australia and the United States by email.

There is a poignant coda. Douglas Blumeyer, who would become Sagittal's next steward, joined the forum in February 2020 and spent a weekend deep in the system. He logged on that Monday intending to contact George for the very first time — and instead learned that George had died. They never spoke.
