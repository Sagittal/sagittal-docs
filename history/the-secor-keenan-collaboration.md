# The Secor–Keenan Collaboration

Sagittal is the work of two people who **never met face to face**. George Secor lived in the United States; Dave Keenan lives in Australia. For **eighteen years** they developed the notation together, almost entirely by email — a slow, careful correspondence that produced one of the most complete pitch-notation systems ever devised.

## A division of genius and craft

Dave has always been generous, and precise, about the shape of the partnership:

> "Sagittal is the creation of George's genius. I merely acted as midwife." — Dave Keenan

That is characteristically modest, but it names a real division of labor. George supplied the founding vision and much of the deep theory. Dave systematized, tested, and extended it — building the spreadsheets, refining the symbol sets, running the arithmetic, and pinning down the thousands of small decisions a universal notation requires. Between them they debated, cross-checked, and slowly converged, over years, on the system we have today.

Many others contributed along the way, especially in the early Yahoo tuning-group years, and George and Dave were always careful to acknowledge them. But the sustained, obsessive work of design was theirs.

## The succession

George Secor died in March 2020. By an accident of timing, that was almost exactly when Douglas Blumeyer first wrote to Dave — obsessed with Sagittal, wanting to build a notation calculator, and eager to understand every decision behind the system.

Dave recognized the moment for what it was. He knew Sagittal's full rationale was carried in his own memory and in decades of private email, and that only a handful of people — Graham Breed and Herman Miller among them — understood the system deeply enough to notate an arbitrary tuning well. None had shown the particular obsession that he and George had shared, and that Douglas now plainly did.

So Dave took Douglas on as collaborator and successor. Later that year, writing to Steinberg in the course of the SMuFL standardization work, he put the handoff in writing:

> "Douglas knows more about Sagittal than anyone else on the planet. If anything should happen to me, he has full authority to make any decisions regarding Sagittal standards." — Dave Keenan

## How they work

The collaboration settled into a rhythm: email, plus at most one video call a week (Dave's stated preference). A recurring practice was **independent cross-checking** — Douglas's JavaScript codebase and Dave's Excel spreadsheets computing the same results by different means, each validating the other.

The roles sorted themselves out naturally:

* **Dave** is the authority on design rationale and history, the conservator of George's final decisions (minimizing changes to the JI notation, and sitting on any proposed change "for three months to a year" before acting), the forum administrator, and the steward of the domain and server.
* **Douglas** is the software and tooling builder — the calculators, StaffCode, and the code that generates Sagittal's data — and the front-facing community editor across the Xenharmonic wiki, Discord, Facebook, this documentation site, and the tutorial video series.

Their shared mission, in Dave's words, is to "have some fun with this thing we've been bequeathed" — and, in doing so, to ensure that George's effort, and Dave's, is not wasted. This wiki is one product of that mission.
