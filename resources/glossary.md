# Glossary

Concise definitions of Sagittal terms, cross-linked. Names of symbol sets, size categories, and flavors are capitalized where they are proper names.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Pronunciation guide.** _Sagittal_ = one "g," two "t's." _apotome_ = uh-POT-uh-mee. _diesis_ = DEE-uh-sis. _limma_ = LIM-uh. _secor_ = SEE-kor.
{% endhint %}

### accent

One of three types of symbol [elements](glossary.md#element), along with [shafts](glossary.md#shaft) and [flags](glossary.md#flag). Also called a **diacritic**.

The [core](glossary.md#core) of a sagittal consists of its shafts and usually at least one flag. Everything besides the core is an accent. Accents always represent smaller pitch alterations than shafts and flags, so they are always found to the left of the core, and are graphically smaller. Unlike flags, accents may point in different directions from the flags and from each other. The [Herculean](glossary.md#herculean) set introduces the first accents (the acute and grave); [Olympian](glossary.md#olympian) introduces the breves.

### apotome

The Pythagorean **chromatic semitone**, [-11 7⟩ = 2187:2048 ≈ 113.685¢ — the interval from a nominal to its sharp, equal to a chain of seven fifths octave-reduced. Used generically for the chromatic semitone of any temperament. Pronounced uh-POT-uh-mee; from Greek "the part cut away."

### apotome complement

The sagittal that, added to a given sagittal, sums to an [apotome](glossary.md#apotome) (a sharp). Because every 2,3-free ratio has two commas that differ by an apotome, a symbol plus its apotome complement makes a sharp. In [Revo](glossary.md#revo), complements must be memorized — there is no simple rule.

### arc

A curved type of [flag](glossary.md#flag), and thus a symbol [element](glossary.md#element).

### Athenian

The name of the second [symbol set](glossary.md#symbol-set), and thus the first [extension](glossary.md#extension) beyond [Spartan](glossary.md#spartan-symbol-set). Corresponds to **High** [precision](glossary.md#precision-level).

### barb

A straight type of [flag](glossary.md#flag), and thus a symbol [element](glossary.md#element). The left barb is the 5-comma; the right barb is the 55-comma.

### boathook

A type of [flag](glossary.md#flag), introduced in the later [symbol sets](glossary.md#symbol-set).

### capture zone

The contiguous, non-overlapping range of interval sizes that a single symbol is used to notate. Within its capture zone a symbol represents whatever [comma](glossary.md#comma) falls there, defaulting to its [primary comma](glossary.md#primary-comma). See also [secondary comma zone](glossary.md#secondary-comma-zone).

### chromatic semitone

The interval from a nominal to its sharp — a chain of seven fifths, octave-reduced, about 114¢ in Pythagorean tuning. Its Pythagorean value is the [apotome](glossary.md#apotome). Contrast the [diatonic semitone](glossary.md#limma).

### comma

The word has two meanings in Sagittal:

1. the general term for any of the small pitch intervals that Sagittal symbols represent;
2. the specific term for one of the seven [size categories](glossary.md#comma-size-categories) — the one exactly in the middle, with three on either side.

The category _comma_ runs from 11.730¢ to 33.382¢. See [Comma Size Categories](../concept-explanations/comma-sizes.md).

### comma size categories

The seven categories, by size only, from smallest to largest: [schismina](glossary.md#schismina), [schisma](glossary.md#schisma), [kleisma](glossary.md#kleisma), [comma](glossary.md#comma), small [diesis](glossary.md#diesis), medium diesis, and large diesis. Each boundary is the square root of a meaningful 3-limit comma.

### core

The part of a sagittal consisting of just the [shaft](glossary.md#shaft) and [flags](glossary.md#flag), if any (excluding [accents](glossary.md#accent)).

### diacritic

See [accent](glossary.md#accent).

### diatonic semitone

See [limma](glossary.md#limma).

### diesis

Both a small interval and a family of three [size categories](glossary.md#comma-size-categories) — small, medium, and large diesis — larger than a [comma](glossary.md#comma) and smaller than a semitone. Pronounced DEE-uh-sis.

### EDA

**Equal Division of the Apotome.** Each nested [symbol set](glossary.md#symbol-set) approximates an EDA of increasing resolution. The [tina](glossary.md#tina) is one step of the 809-EDA.

### element

Short for _symbol element_. Every sagittal is built from at least two elements. Three types exist: [shafts](glossary.md#shaft), [flags](glossary.md#flag), and [accents](glossary.md#accent). No sagittal has more than four shafts, three flags, or three accents. Shafts and flags together form the [core](glossary.md#core).

### element arithmetic

For every flag or accent there is a sagittal consisting of a single shaft plus that element. Element arithmetic combines those elements' [primary commas](glossary.md#primary-comma) (in tempered form, if relevant) to give a sagittal's [sum-of-elements](glossary.md#sum-of-elements) value. [Flag arithmetic](../concept-explanations/flag-arithmetic.md) is the special case using only flags.

### Evo

One of the two [flavors](glossary.md#flavor) of Sagittal, short for "evolutionary." Evo uses single-shaft sagittals combined with the conventional sharp and flat symbols. It is the pedagogical stepping-stone, taught before [Revo](glossary.md#revo). Formerly called **Mixed**.

### extension

Any [symbol set](glossary.md#symbol-set) beyond the base [Spartan](glossary.md#spartan-symbol-set) set.

### flag

One of three types of symbol [elements](glossary.md#element), along with [shafts](glossary.md#shaft) and [accents](glossary.md#accent). There are eight flag types: the left and right versions of the [barb](glossary.md#barb), [arc](glossary.md#arc), [scroll](glossary.md#scroll), and [boathook](glossary.md#boathook). A sagittal is often named by its flags.

### flavor

Sagittal comes in two flavors, [Evo](glossary.md#evo) and [Revo](glossary.md#revo). They are equally preferred but must never be mixed within a score.

### Herculean

The fourth [symbol set](glossary.md#symbol-set); the first to introduce [accents](glossary.md#accent) (the tick).

### kleisma

The third-smallest [comma size category](glossary.md#comma-size-categories), from 4.500¢ to 11.730¢.

### limma

The Pythagorean **diatonic semitone**, [8 -5⟩ = 256:243 ≈ 90.225¢ — a chain of five fifths downward, octave-reduced. In the size-category scheme it is a **medium semitone**. From Greek "a remnant" — what remains of a whole tone after the [apotome](glossary.md#apotome) is cut away.

### Magrathean

The largest and highest-resolution [symbol set](glossary.md#symbol-set), reaching [tina](glossary.md#tina)-level precision.

### mina

A precision unit at the [Olympian](glossary.md#olympian) level: there are **233 minas per [apotome](glossary.md#apotome)** (about 0.5¢ each). Short for _schismina_-scale mina.

### Mixed

See [Evo](glossary.md#evo).

### monzo

See [prime count vector](glossary.md#prime-count-vector). Named for microtonalist [Joseph Monzo](http://www.tonalsoft.com/enc/m/monzo-writings.aspx). Sagittal materials often prefer the plainer term _prime count vector_ (or _prime exponent vector_).

### native-fifth notation

An EDO notation that uses the fifth native to that EDO, as opposed to a [subset notation](glossary.md#subset-notation), which borrows a larger EDO's fifth.

### nominal

One of the notational letter names — the standard set of seven (A B C D E F G) corresponding to the white keys of a keyboard, arranged along a chain of fifths centered on D.

### Olympian

The fifth [symbol set](glossary.md#symbol-set), reaching [mina](glossary.md#mina)-level (Extreme) [precision](glossary.md#precision-level); introduces the breve accents.

### precision level

How finely a notation distinguishes pitches, corresponding to which [symbol set](glossary.md#symbol-set) is used. The four JI precision levels are **Medium**, **High**, **Ultra**, and **Extreme**, corresponding to Athenian, Promethean, Herculean, and Olympian. (The published *Xenharmonikôn* article uses a coarser three-level scheme — Medium, High, Extreme — with Medium likewise being Athenian; see the [JI precision levels](../reference/ji-precision-levels.md) table.) A symbol keeps the same default value at every level (`/|` is always 81:80).

### primary comma

The default [comma](glossary.md#comma) value of a sagittal — what the symbol means unless the score says otherwise.

### prime count vector

Also called a _harmonic space coordinate_, _prime exponent vector_, or [monzo](glossary.md#monzo). It expresses a rational number by how many of each prime it contains: each term is the exponent of the corresponding prime (2, 3, 5, 7, …). For example, 81/80 = 2⁻⁴ · 3⁴ · 5⁻¹ = \| -4 4 -1 ⟩. Trailing zeros may be dropped.

### Prime Factor notation

A [just intonation](../notation-how-to-guides/just-intonation.md) notation using one sagittal per prime factor of a ratio. Easy to learn and close to HEJI/Johnston, but a single note may carry several opposing sagittals — which motivates Sagittal's preferred [JI notation](../notation-how-to-guides/just-intonation.md).

### Promethean

The third [symbol set](glossary.md#symbol-set). Several of its 19-limit symbols include the left scroll, nicknamed "the key to the underworld."

### Pure

See [Revo](glossary.md#revo).

### Revo

One of the two [flavors](glossary.md#flavor) of Sagittal, short for "revolutionary." Revo abandons the conventional sharp and flat symbols, using multi-shaft sagittals for every alteration — one accidental per note. Formerly called **Pure**: "if you can't do it in Pure Sagittal, it doesn't exist."

### sagittal

A lowercase _sagittal_ is a symbol in the uppercase _Sagittal_ notation system. It consists of a [core](glossary.md#core) (one [shaft](glossary.md#shaft) and zero or more [flags](glossary.md#flag)) plus zero or more [accents](glossary.md#accent).

### Sagispeak

The spelling and pronunciation scheme for Sagittal symbols. Direction sets the ending vowel ("ai" up, "ao" down); flags set the consonants. See [Sagispeak](../concept-explanations/sagispeak.md).

### sagitype

The scheme for typing sagittals in ASCII, mapping each element to a keyboard character (`/` `\` barbs, `(` `)` `~` curved flags, `|` up-shaft, `!` down-shaft, accents by quote marks). See [Sagitype](../concept-explanations/sagitype.md).

### schisma

The second-smallest [comma size category](glossary.md#comma-size-categories), from 1.808¢ to 4.500¢.

### schismina

The smallest [comma size category](glossary.md#comma-size-categories), from the [unison](glossary.md#unison) (exclusive) up to 1.808¢.

### scroll

A type of [flag](glossary.md#flag). The left scroll appears in [Promethean](glossary.md#promethean) 19-limit symbols.

### secondary comma

A non-default [comma](glossary.md#comma) that a sagittal may represent in a given tuning or context, as opposed to its [primary comma](glossary.md#primary-comma).

### secondary comma zone

The range of interval sizes, around a symbol's [primary comma](glossary.md#primary-comma), within which that symbol captures other commas. See [capture zone](glossary.md#capture-zone).

### secor

The generator of Miracle temperament, roughly a 16:15 minor second. Named after (not by) George Secor; the mnemonic is **SEC**ond min**OR**. Pronounced SEE-kor.

### shaft

One of three types of symbol [elements](glossary.md#element), along with [flags](glossary.md#flag) and [accents](glossary.md#accent). The vertical stroke of a sagittal, indicating direction: `|` points up, `!` points down. A sagittal has one to four shafts; multi-shaft symbols belong to [Revo](glossary.md#revo).

### Spartan symbol set

The base [symbol set](glossary.md#symbol-set) — the smallest, most common set, corresponding to **Medium** [precision](glossary.md#precision-level). Includes the starter set `/|`, `|)`, `/|\`.

### subset notation

A non-native-fifth EDO notation that treats the EDO as a subset of a larger EDO and samples that EDO's notation — e.g. 8-EDO as a subset of 24-EDO. Used for EDOs whose own fifth is too badly tuned. Contrast [native-fifth notation](glossary.md#native-fifth-notation).

### sum-of-elements

The value obtained by performing [element arithmetic](glossary.md#element-arithmetic) on a single sagittal's [elements](glossary.md#element).

### symbol set

Sagittal's symbols are organized into seven nested sets of increasing [precision](glossary.md#precision-level): [Spartan](glossary.md#spartan-symbol-set), [Athenian](glossary.md#athenian), [Promethean](glossary.md#promethean), [Herculean](glossary.md#herculean), [Olympian](glossary.md#olympian), [Magrathean](glossary.md#magrathean), plus the askew [Trojan](glossary.md#trojan) set for [12-Relative](../notation-how-to-guides/12-relative-trojan.md) notation. See [Symbol Sets](../concept-explanations/symbol-sets.md).

### tina

The finest precision unit: there are **809 tinas per [apotome](glossary.md#apotome)** (about 0.14¢ each) — one step of the 809-[EDA](glossary.md#eda). The [Magrathean](glossary.md#magrathean) set works at tina resolution.

### Trojan

The askew [symbol set](glossary.md#symbol-set) used for [12-Relative](../notation-how-to-guides/12-relative-trojan.md) notation, where the apotome is exactly 100¢. Hence 12-Relative is also called **Trojan** notation.

### unison

The interval 1/1 — no move at all. Written **1u** in the comma-naming scheme. Not really a [size category](glossary.md#comma-size-categories); it is a single point, since all category boundaries are exclusive.

### whole tone

An interval the size of one [apotome](glossary.md#apotome) plus one [limma](glossary.md#limma) — a chain of two fifths, about 204¢.
